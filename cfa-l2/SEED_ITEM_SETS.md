## EQUITY (Days 2–4)
**Vignette.** Arjun Mehta, CFA, values Kestrel Foods. For the year: net income $100m, depreciation $30m, interest expense $20m, tax rate 25%, fixed-capital investment $50m, working-capital investment $10m, net borrowing $15m. Shares outstanding 50m. Mehta uses required return on equity 10%, WACC 8%, and a long-run growth rate 4%. Separately, Kestrel's payout ratio is 40% and ROE is 12%.

1. FCFF is *closest to*: A. $70m  B. $85m  C. $100m
2. FCFE is *closest to*: A. $70m  B. $85m  C. $115m
3. Using single-stage FCFE (g = 4%), value per share is *closest to*: A. $17.70  B. $29.50  C. $31.20
4. Kestrel's sustainable growth rate is *closest to*: A. 4.0%  B. 7.2%  C. 12.0%
5. Justified leading P/E (using g = 7.2%) is *closest to*: A. 5.6  B. 10.0  C. 14.3
6. Because ROE (12%) exceeds required return (10%), residual income is positive, implying justified P/B is: A. <1  B. =1  C. >1

**Key.** 1-**B** (100+30+20×.75−50−10 = 85). 2-**B** (85−15+15 = 85; or 100+30−50−10+15). 3-**B** (85×1.04/(.10−.04)=1473.3m ÷ 50 = $29.47). 4-**B** (g = b×ROE = .6×.12 = 7.2%). 5-**C** ((1−b)/(r−g)=.4/.028 = 14.3). 6-**C** (ROE>r → RI>0 → P/B>1).

## FIXED INCOME (Days 5–7)
**Vignette.** The 1-year spot rate is 3.00% and the 2-year spot rate is 4.00% (annual compounding). Lena Voss analyzes a callable bond priced at 100.0; when the yield curve shifts down 25 bp its price is 101.8, and up 25 bp its price is 98.5. Assume interest-rate volatility then increases.

1. The 1-year forward rate one year from now, f(1,1), is *closest to*: A. 3.50%  B. 5.01%  C. 6.00%
2. The value of the callable bond equals: A. straight bond + call value  B. straight bond − call value  C. straight bond + put value
3. For this callable bond, the OAS versus the Z-spread is: A. OAS > Z-spread  B. OAS = Z-spread  C. OAS < Z-spread
4. The bond's effective duration is *closest to*: A. 3.3  B. 6.6  C. 13.2
5. After volatility rises, the callable bond's value and its OAS (holding price process fixed) will: A. both rise  B. value falls, OAS falls  C. value rises, OAS rises
6. The appropriate duration measure for a bond with an embedded option is: A. Macaulay  B. modified  C. effective

**Key.** 1-**B** (1.04²/1.03 − 1 = 5.01%). 2-**B**. 3-**C** (call option cost is positive → OAS below Z-spread). 4-**B** ((101.8−98.5)/(2×100×.0025)=6.6). 5-**B** (higher vol → call worth more → callable value falls; computed OAS falls). 6-**C**.

## DERIVATIVES (Days 8–9)
**Vignette.** Discount factors: 1-yr 0.97, 2-yr 0.93, 3-yr 0.89. For a stock at $100, a one-period binomial model uses up factor u = 1.20, down factor d = 0.833, and a one-period risk-free rate of 2%. A European call has strike $100.

1. The fixed rate on a 3-year annual-pay interest-rate swap is *closest to*: A. 3.94%  B. 4.30%  C. 5.10%
2. The risk-neutral probability of an up move is *closest to*: A. 0.49  B. 0.51  C. 0.55
3. The call value today is *closest to*: A. $8.0  B. $10.0  C. $12.0
4. The hedge ratio (delta) of the call is *closest to*: A. 0.36  B. 0.55  C. 0.80
5. In the BSM model, N(d1) is best interpreted as: A. probability the option expires ITM  B. the option's delta / shares in the replicating portfolio  C. the present value factor
6. Using put-call parity, the value of a European put with the same strike is *closest to*: A. $6.0  B. $8.0  C. $10.0

**Key.** 1-**A** ((1−0.89)/(0.97+0.93+0.89)=0.11/2.79=3.94%). 2-**B** ((1.02−0.833)/(1.20−0.833)=0.51). 3-**B** ([0.51×20+0.49×0]/1.02≈10.0). 4-**B** ((20−0)/(120−83.3)=0.545). 5-**B**. 6-**B** (p = c + X/(1+r) − S = 10 + 98.04 − 100 = 8.04).

## FSA (Days 16–18) — pensions
**Vignette.** Nadia Sharma reviews a defined-benefit plan (US GAAP). Beginning PBO $1,000m; current service cost $80m; discount rate 5%; benefits paid $40m; actuarial loss $30m. Plan assets: beginning $900m; actual return $60m; employer contributions $100m; benefits paid $40m.

1. Ending PBO is *closest to*: A. $1,090m  B. $1,120m  C. $1,150m
2. Ending fair value of plan assets is *closest to*: A. $1,020m  B. $1,060m  C. $960m
3. The plan's funded status at year-end is *closest to*: A. +$100m  B. −$100m  C. −$60m
4. An increase in the discount rate would, all else equal: A. increase the PBO  B. decrease the PBO and reduce underfunding  C. have no effect on the PBO
5. Under US GAAP, actuarial gains/losses (remeasurements) are reported in: A. net income  B. other comprehensive income  C. retained earnings directly
6. Total periodic pension cost for the year is *closest to*: A. $100m  B. $130m  C. $70m

**Key.** 1-**B** (1000+80+50−40+30). 2-**A** (900+60+100−40). 3-**B** (1020−1120). 4-**B**. 5-**B** (OCI; note service cost + net interest hit P&L). 6-**A** (contributions − Δfunded status = 100 − 0; check: 80+50−60+30 = 100).

## QUANT (Days 14–15) — multiple regression
**Vignette.** A regression of a fund's excess return on two factors uses n = 60 observations. ANOVA: SST = 100, SSR = 45, SSE = 55. One slope coefficient b = 0.80 with standard error 0.25. The Durbin-Watson statistic is 1.20.

1. The R² and adjusted R² are *closest to*: A. 0.45 and 0.43  B. 0.55 and 0.52  C. 0.45 and 0.40
2. The F-statistic is *closest to*, and the regression is: A. 23.3; significant  B. 11.6; not significant  C. 2.0; not significant
3. The t-statistic on the slope (df = 57) indicates the coefficient is: A. insignificant  B. significant at 5% (t = 3.2)  C. significant only at 10%
4. A Durbin-Watson of 1.20 most likely indicates: A. no serial correlation  B. positive serial correlation  C. negative serial correlation
5. The main consequence of that problem for OLS is: A. coefficients biased  B. standard errors understated, t-stats inflated  C. R² understated
6. High R² and a significant F-test but insignificant individual t-stats is the classic symptom of: A. heteroskedasticity  B. serial correlation  C. multicollinearity

**Key.** 1-**A** (R²=45/100=.45; adj = 1−(59/57)(.55)=0.43). 2-**A** ((45/2)/(55/57)=23.3; >crit → significant). 3-**B** (0.80/0.25=3.2 > 2.0). 4-**B** (DW well below 2). 5-**B**. 6-**C**.

## ECONOMICS (Day 11) — FX & parity
**Vignette.** Spot USD/EUR = 1.1000 (USD per EUR). One-year interest rates: US 4.0%, Eurozone 2.0%. Expected one-year inflation: US 3.0%, Eurozone 1.0%.

1. The one-year forward USD/EUR from covered interest rate parity is *closest to*: A. 1.0784  B. 1.1216  C. 1.1000
2. Relative to spot, the EUR trades at a forward: A. premium  B. discount  C. neither
3. Under uncovered IRP, the EUR is expected to: A. appreciate ~2%  B. depreciate ~2%  C. stay flat
4. Under relative PPP, the expected change in USD/EUR is *closest to*: A. −2% (EUR depreciates)  B. +2% (EUR appreciates)  C. 0%
5. A carry trade here would borrow ___ and invest in ___: A. USD; EUR  B. EUR; USD  C. neither — rates are equal
6. Which parity condition is enforced by arbitrage and therefore (essentially) always holds? A. uncovered IRP  B. purchasing power parity  C. covered IRP

**Key.** 1-**B** (1.10×1.04/1.02=1.1216). 2-**A** (forward > spot; lower-rate currency at premium). 3-**A** (higher-rate USD expected to depreciate → EUR appreciates ~2%). 4-**B** (inflation differential 3−1 = +2%). 5-**B** (borrow low-yield EUR, invest high-yield USD). 6-**C**.

## PORTFOLIO MANAGEMENT (Days 12–13) — active management & VaR
**Vignette.** A manager has information coefficient IC = 0.05, breadth BR = 100 independent decisions/year, transfer coefficient TC = 0.80, and targets active risk of 4%. The fund also reports a 5%, one-day parametric VaR of $1,000,000.

1. The manager's information ratio is *closest to*: A. 0.05  B. 0.40  C. 0.50
2. Expected active return is *closest to*: A. 0.5%  B. 1.6%  C. 4.0%
3. If breadth rises to 400 (IC, TC unchanged), the information ratio becomes *closest to*: A. 0.40  B. 0.60  C. 0.80
4. The VaR figure means: A. the fund will lose exactly $1m tomorrow  B. there is a 5% chance of losing at least $1m in one day  C. maximum possible loss is $1m
5. A key limitation of parametric (variance-covariance) VaR is that it: A. requires huge samples  B. assumes normally distributed returns, understating tail risk  C. cannot be computed for equities
6. A transfer coefficient of 1.0 would imply: A. no constraints limit implementing the manager's insights  B. the manager has no skill  C. maximum diversification

**Key.** 1-**B** (0.05×√100×0.80 = 0.40). 2-**B** (0.40×4% = 1.6%). 3-**C** (√400 = 20 → 0.05×20×0.8 = 0.80). 4-**B**. 5-**B**. 6-**A**.

## ALTERNATIVE INVESTMENTS (Day 10)
**Vignette.** A commercial property generates stabilized NOI of $500,000; the market cap rate is 8%. A REIT reports net income $200m, real-estate depreciation $150m, and gains on property sales $30m. A commodities fund holds futures in a market currently in contango.

1. The property value by direct capitalization is *closest to*: A. $4.0m  B. $5.0m  C. $6.25m
2. If the cap rate rises to 10%, the value becomes *closest to*: A. $5.0m  B. $6.25m  C. $7.5m
3. The REIT's funds from operations (FFO) is *closest to*: A. $320m  B. $350m  C. $380m
4. Because the futures market is in contango, the roll return is most likely: A. positive  B. negative  C. zero
5. A merger-arbitrage strategy (long target, short acquirer) is classified as: A. relative value  B. event-driven  C. global macro
6. Holding the discount rate constant, higher expected NOI growth implies a ___ cap rate and a ___ value: A. higher; lower  B. lower; higher  C. lower; lower

**Key.** 1-**C** (500,000/0.08). 2-**A** (500,000/0.10; inverse relation). 3-**A** (200+150−30). 4-**B** (contango → sell low-price expiring, buy higher-price next → negative roll). 5-**B**. 6-**B** (cap rate = discount − growth).

## ETHICS (Day 19) — application
**Vignette.** Vikram Rao, CFA, works at an asset manager. (1) In a client's elevator he overhears the client's CFO say a takeover will be announced next week; Rao buys the stock that afternoon. (2) His marketing deck shows "model" backtested returns labeled simply "composite performance." (3) He recommends a leveraged product to a conservative retiree without documenting a suitability review. (4) A client privately offers Rao a beach-house week if the portfolio beats its benchmark; Rao accepts without telling his employer.

1. Rao's purchase after the elevator remark most directly violates: A. I(C) Misrepresentation  B. II(A) Material Nonpublic Information  C. III(B) Fair Dealing
2. Labeling backtested model returns as "composite performance" violates: A. III(D) Performance Presentation  B. IV(A) Loyalty  C. VI(A) Disclosure of Conflicts
3. Combining public data with pieces of nonmaterial nonpublic information to reach a conclusion is: A. always prohibited  B. permitted under the mosaic theory  C. permitted only with client consent
4. Recommending the leveraged product to the retiree without a suitability review violates: A. III(C) Suitability  B. V(A) Diligence only  C. no standard, since the client can decline
5. To control the MNPI problem firm-wide, the best practice is to: A. increase trade volume  B. establish information barriers (a firewall)  C. disclose all trades publicly
6. Accepting the beach-house incentive from the client without disclosure most directly violates: A. IV(B) Additional Compensation Arrangements  B. III(A) Loyalty, Prudence and Care  C. I(B) Independence and Objectivity — and requires written consent of the employer

**Key.** 1-**B**. 2-**A** (also I(C)). 3-**B**. 4-**A**. 5-**B**. 6-**A** (must get written consent from employer before accepting compensation contingent on performance; I(B) is also implicated).
