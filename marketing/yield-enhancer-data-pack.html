Exhibit 4 · Why short duration

Rising rates hurt long bonds. A 1–1.5 year book barely feels them.

Bond prices fall roughly by duration × rise in yield. In 2026 the 10Y G-Sec yield rose about 100 bps from its low. A 1.25-year portfolio absorbs that in about a month of coupon, and its maturing bonds get reinvested at the new, higher yields.

Exhibit 6 · Predictability

Equity’s average looks great. The year you need the money may not.

In 8 of the last 18 calendar years the Nifty 50 returned less than 10% (price return), and in 3 it lost money. 2026 is on track to be the 4th. Equity has averaged about 11.6% a year since 2008, almost exactly the 11% target, yet only 2 of 18 years actually ended near 11%.

Guardrails

Say this carefully

An 11% target in 1–1.5 year paper is well above AAA (~8%) and FD (6.25%) rates. That premium is payment for credit and liquidity risk, typically A-category or structured paper (indicative 9.5–11.5%+ before this year’s ~100 bps rise in G-Sec yields). Clients who remember IL&FS (2018), DHFL (2019) and the 2020 Franklin Templeton debt-fund wind-up will ask. Lead with the diligence story and the campaign holds up.

Say

  • “Target return of 11% p.a.; returns are not assured.”
  • “Lower interest-rate sensitivity than long-duration bonds.”
  • “Predictable cash flows from coupons and maturities.”
  • “Credit selected through [N]-step due diligence.”

Avoid

  • “Safe”, “guaranteed”, “assured”, “FD-like”, “risk-free”
  • Comparing to FDs without noting FDs carry DICGC cover up to ₹5 lakh
  • Showing target returns as if they were past performance
  • “Indian markets are overvalued” (Nifty 50 P/E is below median)

Before anything goes out

  • Compliance sign-off against SEBI advertisement norms for the product’s structure (PMS / AIF)
  • Risk-o-meter / risk disclosures and “past performance…” language
  • Verify every market number on this page against NSE, RBI, NSDL, MoSPI on the day of publication
