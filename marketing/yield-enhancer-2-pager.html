Equity is built for long horizons and remains the engine of long-term wealth. Money with a job in the next 12–18 months needs a return that does not depend on market timing. Most investors need both.

What this means for your portfolio

Give each rupee the right job

Keep long-term money in equity. Put 12–18 month money where returns come from contractual coupons, not market mood.

Short beats long when rates rise

Long bonds lose value as yields climb. A 1–1.5 year portfolio barely moves and reinvests at the new, higher yields.

Deposits are losing after tax

In the 30% bracket, a 6.25% FD keeps 4.3%. With inflation at 5.5%, that is a loss in real terms.

Why earn a credit premium at the short end

1A shorter window to judge. It is far easier to assess whether a company can pay over 12–18 months than over ten years.
2Capital comes back sooner. Maturing bonds are reinvested at today’s higher yields, which helps in a rising-rate cycle.
3Little rate sensitivity. Returns come mainly from coupons, not from betting on where interest rates go.

Know the risks

Credit risk. An issuer may delay or miss payments. We manage this through issuer due diligence and diversification.

Liquidity risk. Corporate bonds can be hard to sell before maturity. Invest money you will not need for at least 12–18 months.

Not a bank deposit. No DICGC deposit insurance; capital is not guaranteed.
